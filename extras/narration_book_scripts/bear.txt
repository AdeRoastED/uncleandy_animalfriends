Bo's Morning — 1

Bo woke up with a very serious problem: his stomach was making noises louder than his own growl. “I think there's a bear living in my tummy,” he announced. Mother laughed and led him through the autumn forest. Brown bears eat many different foods, and at this time of year they need plenty of energy before winter.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/bear-page-03.mp3
Bo's Morning — 2

Near a fallen tree, Bo caught the delicious smell of ripe berries. The log was covered in green moss, with two little branches sticking up like ears. “A sleeping green bear!” he cried. Mother showed him the familiar berries she ate, and Bo carefully picked several of the ripe ones with his lips.

A leaf landed on Bo's nose while he was choosing a berry. He shook it away, then noticed a tiny beetle disappearing into the moss. The fallen tree was not simply a strange green shape beside their picnic. It was a home for little creatures, a place for moss to grow and a landmark he could recognise. Bo decided to remember its two little branch ears rather than trying to remember every tree in the forest. He could hardly believe how much was happening around a place he had only noticed because he was hungry.

Bo's Morning — 3

A thrush fluttered onto a branch above them. Bo watched it swallow a berry and fly away. There was enough fruit for many visitors. Mother moved on to search for other food, and Bo memorised the mossy log. “Goodbye, green bear. Guard our picnic!” he said, trotting after her.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/bear-page-05.mp3
Bo's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon, Bo's tummy was making its famous noise again. He hoped they could return to the berries, but the forest paths looked remarkably alike. A log lay across one path, another near a tree. None had two little mossy ears. Bo wanted to rush into the bushes to investigate every smell.

Bo sniffed at one log, then at another. Both smelled interesting, but neither was the green bear he remembered. The autumn forest seemed to have filled itself with confusing little paths. He felt impatient and wanted to follow every berry scent at once. Mother stayed close and let him take a moment to look. Bo remembered the big oak near the mossy log, and how the two branch ears had pointed towards the sky. There was no need to rush into unfamiliar bushes. Perhaps the forest would look less confusing if he noticed one detail at a time.

Bo's Afternoon — 2

Mother waited while he stopped and looked. A familiar oak stood near the path, and beyond it he glimpsed the green log. “There!” he exclaimed. They approached the berry patch together. Bo was so pleased that he began munching enthusiastically, but noticed that some branches bent beneath his large paws.

Afternoon · page 3

Bo's Afternoon — 3

He shifted onto a clear patch of ground and ate from the edge instead. A small bird landed on a branch nearby, looking for its own meal. Bo left the fruit on that side untouched. Mother found another ripe cluster, and they shared the peaceful picnic without flattening the bushes around them.

From the clear ground, Bo could reach the ripe fruit without stepping on the smaller branches. The thrush hopped closer to its own part of the bush, swallowed a berry and fluttered away again. Bo watched it with great interest. His own mouth could hold an enormous mouthful compared with the bird's little beak. They were sharing the same patch in very different ways. He found another delicious berry near the edge, and Mother moved on when they had eaten enough. Behind them the bush still stood, with plenty of fruit shining among its leaves.

Bo's Evening — 1

As evening approached, the thrush returned with several other birds. Bo sat at a distance and watched them pluck the remaining berries. He realised the patch wasn't a secret cupboard made especially for him. It was part of a busy forest, where different creatures needed food for different reasons.

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/bear-page-09.mp3
Bo's Evening — 2

Mother and Bo continued towards their resting place, where dry leaves rustled beneath their paws. “Did I find the best picnic in the forest?” Bo asked. “You found a good one,” Mother smiled. “And you left it good for the next visitors.” Bo thought that sounded even better than being the best finder.

Bo looked back at the path and imagined the green bear guarding its picnic through the autumn. A bird called from the branches, and he thought of the little visitors who would be back after he had gone. He had discovered a good place to eat without needing to keep it all for himself. Mother walked beside him through the leaves, their paws making soft crunching sounds. Bo was already beginning to feel sleepy. The forest seemed like a very large home, with many different creatures making use of its corners in their own ways.

Bo's Evening — 3

Under the darkening trees, Bo curled up with his warm fur against Mother. Soon winter would bring a long rest, but tonight was just an ordinary, lovely autumn night. His stomach gave one last tiny rumble. “Goodnight, tummy bear,” he whispered, and dreamed of a green mossy bear surrounded by birds.
