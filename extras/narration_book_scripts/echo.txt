Echo's Morning

“Hrrumph!” Echo announced, lifting her enormous nose out of the river. A little egret jumped in surprise. “Good morning to you too!” Echo chuckled. The African sun was already painting the water gold, and her family lay in the shallows like a collection of enormous, rather sleepy stones. Only their eyes, ears and nostrils showed above the surface. Echo liked to call this her river hat.

Echo's Morning

The egret was searching for breakfast along the bank. Every time a ripple arrived, its reflection broke into a hundred little pieces. Farther downstream, behind a bend thick with reeds, the water was almost perfectly still. Echo watched the bird step into the sheltered shallows. “A quiet corner in a noisy river,” she murmured. “I wonder why the waves never seem to reach it?”

A dragonfly drew a brilliant blue line across the water, then landed on a reed. Echo tried to follow its reflection with her nose, but her own little ripples broke the picture apart. She stopped moving. Slowly, the river became a mirror again. In the quiet bend, the egret could see straight down to the little fish in the shallows. Echo watched until the bird caught one with a quick jab. How much easier everything seemed when the water was still!

Echo's Morning

Her mother called her back to the family. Echo pushed off the riverbed with her strong feet, bouncing through the water rather than swimming like a fish. “Coming!” she grunted. Hippos spend much of the hot day in water to protect their skin and keep cool. Echo settled beside her mother, flicked a fly away with her ear and closed her eyes. The little bend slipped out of her thoughts.

Echo's Afternoon

By afternoon the wind had arrived. Whoooosh! It shook the reeds and sent little waves slapping against Echo's cheeks. “My river hat is getting rather uncomfortable,” she complained. Then she heard a different sound: a tiny, worried peep. On a low branch near the bank stood a young egret. Its mother had flown around the bend, and the youngster seemed unsure where to go.

The young bird took a step along the branch. It was a very thin branch indeed, and when a gust swept down the river, it bounced like a spring. Echo's ears twitched. She could hear the larger birds calling over the water, but the little one's voice was almost swallowed by the wind. “I have a very big mouth,” she thought. “Perhaps a very big shout would help?” She opened it, then hesitated. The youngster already looked frightened enough.

Echo's Afternoon

Echo wanted to hurry over and help. She took one enormous step, sending a wave rolling towards the branch. The little bird flapped in alarm. Echo froze. “Oh dear. Perhaps enormous feet are not always the answer.” She backed away slowly. Her mother watched from nearby. “Give the little one some room,” she advised. Echo nodded, although doing nothing felt terribly difficult.

Echo's Afternoon

Then a familiar white shape appeared between the reeds. It was the mother egret! Echo remembered the quiet corner from breakfast. She could hear the mother's calls whenever the wind softened. The youngster turned its head, but another gust made it crouch. Echo waited quietly with her family, hoping the wind would settle before darkness arrived.

Echo found a comfortable spot a little upstream from the branch. Each time one of the other hippos shifted, she felt the water stir against her belly. She wondered how the same little wave must look to a bird with feet as thin as twigs. She resisted the temptation to make another splash and watched the reeds instead. Between two gusts, the quiet voice came again. The little bird lifted its head. This time it seemed to know which way to look.

Echo's Evening

As the sun sank, the waves became smaller. The mother egret called again. This time the youngster answered, spread its wings and fluttered towards the sheltered bend. Echo held perfectly still. A moment later two white heads appeared above the reeds. “There you are!” Echo whispered, delighted. She had not needed to carry anyone or make a single enormous splash.

Echo's Evening

The hippos began leaving the river to graze on land, as hippos often do after sunset. Echo followed her mother towards the fresh grass. “I thought helping meant doing something big,” she admitted. Her mother gave a soft grunt. “Sometimes the kindest thing is to make room for someone else.” Echo considered this while chewing a particularly delicious mouthful.

As she grazed, Echo discovered something rather funny. The loudest sound on the bank was not her enormous voice at all, but the ridiculous chomping noises her own family made. She tried chewing silently and lasted about three seconds. Her mother gave her a sideways look. “Being quiet when it matters is quite different from being quiet forever.” Echo swallowed her mouthful and laughed. She liked being a noisy hippo. She liked knowing when not to be one, too.

Echo's Evening

The stars appeared one by one. From the quiet bend came a final little call, and Echo answered with a gentle rumble. She imagined the birds settling safely among the branches. Her own family was close, the grass was sweet, and the evening air felt wonderfully cool. “Goodnight, little voices,” she murmured. The river carried on its soft, endless song.
