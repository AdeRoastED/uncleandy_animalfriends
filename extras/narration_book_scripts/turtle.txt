Tuga's Morning — 1

Tuga woke beneath a ledge where the sea was still blue and sleepy. She stretched one flipper, then another, and rose to the surface for a breath. Above her, a gull shouted as though it had invented the morning. “Good morning to you too!” Tuga called, before diving towards her favourite meadow of seagrass.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/turtle-page-03.mp3
Tuga's Morning — 2

Breakfast was delicious, although Tuga suspected that nobody on land would agree. She was halfway through a particularly tender mouthful when a ribbon of seaweed floated past her nose. She watched it sweep around a rocky point, turn slowly and disappear into a little bay. How curious! The water seemed to have roads that nobody could see.

A little fish darted between the blades of seagrass and then vanished as neatly as if it had closed a green curtain behind itself. Tuga rested near the bottom for a moment and watched the drifting seaweed. On one side of the rock it travelled quickly; on the other side it turned in a slow circle. She rose for another breath, then looked down at the same rock from the surface. It was an excellent landmark, even in a world where the ground and sky seemed to move all the time.

Morning · page 3

Tuga's Morning — 3

Tuga followed the ribbon just far enough to discover a quiet patch behind the rocks. Tiny fish hovered there without being swept away. She surfaced, took another breath and memorised the shape of the point. Then she returned to breakfast. “An excellent morning,” she decided. “Two discoveries and at least seventeen mouthfuls.” She had lost count after twelve.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/turtle-page-05.mp3
Tuga's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon the water was moving much faster. Tuga could see sand drifting along the bottom like smoke. Nearby, a smaller green turtle was paddling furiously towards the meadow but hardly moving at all. Every time he stopped to breathe at the surface, the current carried him farther away. His flippers were beginning to tire.

The young turtle tried another tremendous stroke. His shell moved forwards a little, then slipped backwards. Tuga recognised the look on his face. She had once become so determined to reach a favourite feeding place that she had forgotten to notice which way the water was carrying her. A piece of seaweed swept past them. Instead of fighting it, she watched where it went. There was the rocky point from breakfast, with a calmer patch of water on its far side. Could they reach it without struggling straight against the current?

Tuga's Afternoon — 2

“I'm going backwards!” he complained. “And I'm not even trying to!” Tuga knew the feeling. She began swimming towards him, keeping clear of the strongest flow. She remembered the ribbon of seaweed and the quiet bay. Perhaps they did not have to fight the water at all. Perhaps they could choose a different route.

Afternoon · page 3
Target: audio/bedtime/turtle-page-07.mp3
Tuga's Afternoon — 3

She showed him how to move towards the sheltered side of the rocky point rather than straight against the current. They took their time, surfacing whenever they needed air. At last the water grew calmer. The little turtle stopped paddling and stared in surprise. “My flippers have stopped arguing with the ocean!” he said. Tuga laughed.

From the calmer water, the young turtle looked back at the sandy clouds swirling around the point. He could hardly believe they had been so close to a resting place all along. Tuga showed him another drifting leaf of seagrass, which turned gently instead of racing away. The two rose for a comfortable breath, and the little one finally relaxed his flippers. They waited until the conditions were suitable rather than trying to hurry back. A curious fish inspected their shells and then went about its own mysterious fish business.

Tuga's Evening — 1

As evening approached, the sand settled and the fish returned to their usual hiding places. The two turtles nibbled some seagrass while waiting for the current to ease. Tuga explained that she had discovered this place by watching a piece of seaweed. “A piece of seaweed taught you?” asked the youngster. “Well, it didn't say much,” she replied.

Evening · page 2

Tuga's Evening — 2

When the water became gentler, the little turtle could continue towards his familiar feeding ground. Tuga watched him disappear beyond the meadow, swimming steadily instead of frantically. She felt pleased that a small discovery had helped someone else. Being strong was useful, she thought, but noticing what was happening around you could be even more useful.

Tuga spent a few more minutes exploring the edge of the meadow. In the evening light, every blade of seagrass seemed to wear a little golden ribbon. A group of fish moved with the water and turned together when the current changed. Tuga thought about the young turtle's determined little flippers. They had been working so hard, and yet the water had been working harder. She was glad he had found a gentler route. Tomorrow she would probably discover another of the ocean's invisible roads.

Tuga's Evening — 3

The last golden light slipped through the water. Tuga took a final leisurely breath at the surface and settled beneath her familiar ledge, where she could easily swim up again. The sea whispered against the rocks. Tomorrow there would be more invisible roads to discover. Tonight there was nothing to hurry for, and Tuga rested her tired flippers.
