Pippo's Morning

Pippo woke to a most peculiar sight: a feather was floating past his nest, standing straight up like the sail of a tiny boat. He hurried down to the pond, tripped over his own webbed feet and landed with a magnificent SPLASH. His sister laughed. “Excellent entrance, Captain Pippo!” she called. Pippo bowed, which was rather difficult while floating.

Pippo's Morning

The feather sailed towards a patch of reeds. Pippo paddled after it, watching how the water curled around the stems. Behind them was a quiet little pool, almost completely hidden from the rest of the pond. He could hear the wind above him, but the water here hardly moved. “A secret harbour!” he whispered. His sister was more interested in breakfast.

Pippo circled the sheltered pool. A water beetle drew tiny rings on its surface, and the tall reeds made a kind of green fence between him and the rest of the pond. He could still see his family through the opening, so he didn't wander farther. The feather came to a stop beside a leaf. Pippo gave it a little nudge with his bill. “You are a remarkably clever boat,” he informed it, although the feather, being a feather, did not seem particularly impressed.

Pippo's Morning

They dipped their heads beneath the surface to search for food, their bottoms pointing towards the sky. A frog watched from a lily pad. “Are you two upside down on purpose?” he croaked. Pippo emerged with a dripping beak. “Of course! That's how professional ducks eat.” He shook himself, and droplets rolled off his carefully preened feathers like little silver beads.

Pippo's Afternoon

By afternoon, clouds were gathering. The breeze pushed the floating feather across the pond much faster than before. Pippo tried to race it, but every time he drew close, another gust sent it spinning away. His mother called the young ducks together. “Stay near me, everyone. The wind is getting stronger.” Pippo reluctantly abandoned his race.

As the family gathered, the pond changed colour. The pale clouds turned grey, and little dark ripples chased one another towards the bank. Pippo watched his feather vanish behind a wave and appear again a moment later. His brother was trying very hard to keep up, paddling so quickly that the water bubbled behind him. Pippo's favourite race suddenly looked much less amusing. He wondered whether the feather would disappear entirely, and then wondered something more important: where could the ducks rest until the wind grew quieter?

Pippo's Afternoon

A sudden shower rattled the leaves. The ducks were quite comfortable in the rain, but the open water was becoming choppy. Pippo's smallest brother, who had been paddling happily all morning, was struggling to keep up. “My legs feel like two cooked noodles!” he complained. Pippo slowed down beside him, while Mother Duck gathered the rest of the family.

Pippo's Afternoon

Then Pippo spotted something white bobbing near the reeds. It was his feather! He remembered the sheltered water behind them. “Mother, there's a quiet place over there!” he called. Mother Duck looked carefully and led the family towards the opening. Pippo stayed beside his brother, matching his little strokes instead of racing ahead as he usually did.

They reached the reeds just as another gust swept over the pond. Pippo felt the waves pushing from behind, but Mother Duck was already leading them into the calm opening. The water changed almost immediately. His brother gave a little surprised quack. “It's still raining, but the pond has stopped bouncing!” The feather bobbed against a reed like a tiny flag. Pippo waited until the smallest duck was safely beside the others before he went to look at it. There was room here for the whole family, not just the fastest swimmer.

Pippo's Evening

By evening the rain had softened to a gentle patter. Inside the sheltered pool, the ducks floated comfortably together. Pippo's brother stretched his tired feet. “You could have won your race,” he said. Pippo looked at the feather resting against a reed. “I think I found something better than winning.” His sister grinned. “Captain Pippo and his secret harbour!”

Pippo's Evening

When the wind finally settled, Mother Duck led everyone back towards their resting place. The pond reflected the pink clouds, and the frog was still sitting on his lily pad. Pippo told him about the adventure. “So you discovered a harbour by chasing a feather?” asked the frog. “Exactly,” said Pippo. “Very important scientific duck work.”

The frog puffed up his throat and made a tremendous croak, as if he were announcing Pippo's discovery to the entire pond. Pippo's sister giggled. She had spent the morning thinking the feather was rather boring, but now she wanted to see the hidden pool on another calm day, with Mother close by. Pippo agreed. The feather drifted past them once more, and this time nobody raced it. They let their little boat sail wherever the breeze decided to take it.

Pippo's Evening

The family settled together as the stars appeared. Pippo tucked his bill into his feathers and listened to the last little ripples. He had discovered that paying attention to small things could help someone else, and that staying together mattered more than being first. Somewhere among the reeds, his little feather floated quietly. Goodnight, Captain Pippo. The pond was peaceful again.
