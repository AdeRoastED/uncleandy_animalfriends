Pingo's Morning — 1

Pingo was very proud of his new game. He would take three little steps, flop onto his fluffy tummy and slide across the snow. “One, two, three, wheeee!” he squeaked. His father watched from among the emperor penguins. “Very graceful,” he said. “Especially the bit where you bumped into my feet.”

Pingo's Morning — 2

The Antarctic wind was chilly that morning. Pingo noticed that the grown-ups were standing so close together that he could hardly see the snow between them. At the outside of the group, birds shuffled slowly around. “Why don't they all go straight into the middle?” Pingo asked. “There isn't room for everybody there,” Father explained.

Pingo watched an adult penguin shuffle around the outside of the huddle. It did not push straight through the other birds. Instead, little movements gradually changed which penguins stood nearest the wind. The chicks near their parents looked quite comfortable. Pingo tried to imitate the grown-ups with a very serious shuffle of his own, but his feet were so small that he made hardly any progress. Pebble laughed. “You look like a fluffy little snow cloud.” Pingo decided he was a very distinguished snow cloud indeed.

Pingo's Morning — 3

Father showed him how the penguins changed places little by little. Those facing the wind would eventually reach a warmer spot. Pingo imagined a giant feathery blanket that could walk. He practised making a small gap beside his father, then closing it again. His tiny neighbour, a chick named Pebble, tried too.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/penguin-page-05.mp3
Pingo's Afternoon — 1

After midday, the wind grew louder. Snowflakes swept sideways, and Pingo was glad to stand close to Father. But where was Pebble? He heard a thin, familiar peep from the edge of the group. There stood his friend, with fluffy feathers blowing in every direction. Pingo wanted to run straight over.

The wind lifted loose snow into the air until Pingo could barely see the little marks their feet had made earlier. Pebble was still close to the adult birds, but the gusts were making it difficult to hear one another. Pingo squeezed nearer to Father instead of running away from him. He watched the slowly moving group. He remembered how the grown-ups had made space without pushing. Perhaps, if he stayed near Father and waited for the next little movement, there would be a comfortable place for Pebble as well.

Pingo's Afternoon — 2

“Stay beside me,” Father said, answering the chick's call. Pingo remembered their morning game. There was a little space near his father's feet, but not enough for another chick. He stepped carefully sideways. Father shifted with him, and the neighbouring adults began to shuffle, each making a small adjustment for the next penguin.

Afternoon · page 3

Pingo's Afternoon — 3

Pebble slipped into the opening. “My toes were turning into icicles!” he exclaimed. Pingo giggled. “Mine are still penguin toes, I think.” They stood together while the adults continued their slow movement. When Pingo reached a warmer place, he didn't try to keep it forever. Another penguin needed it next.

The warmth of the group felt very different from standing in the wind. Pingo looked towards the outside and noticed another little chick with its feathers ruffled by the cold. The grown-ups continued shifting slowly, and Pingo moved with them rather than trying to hold his place. Pebble did the same. Neither chick had to be the strongest or the fastest. They simply stayed close to their parents and made room as the group moved. Soon both had warm toes, and Pingo's enormous worry had shrunk to the size of a snowflake.

Pingo's Evening — 1

Towards evening the snow stopped swirling. Beyond the huddle, the ice shone pale blue beneath the low Antarctic sun. Pebble thanked Pingo for making room. “But I only moved a tiny bit,” said Pingo. Father looked around the enormous colony. “A tiny bit from each of us makes a very big difference.”

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/penguin-page-09.mp3
Pingo's Evening — 2

Pingo tried his sliding game again on a small patch of soft snow. This time he waited until Pebble was ready. They slid side by side, two fluffy little bellies making two funny tracks. The adults called them back, and the chicks hurried towards the shelter of their familiar group.

When the little sliding game ended, Pingo looked back at their two trails in the snow. The marks curved towards each other, then disappeared beneath the feet of the penguins. Pebble gave a cheerful little peep. They tucked themselves near their parents again, where the cold wind was much less troublesome. Pingo decided that sliding was an excellent game, but sharing a warm place was an even better one. Perhaps tomorrow they could invent a new game, one that had room for all the little penguins.

Pingo's Evening — 3

At rest, Pingo nestled near Father. He could still hear the penguins calling softly, but the wind no longer seemed so fierce. He closed his eyes and pictured that enormous walking blanket. Its secret wasn't the warmest place in the middle; it was all the penguins looking after one another. Sleep well, little Pingo.
