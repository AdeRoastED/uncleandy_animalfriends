Oli's Morning

Oli opened one eye and discovered a leaf resting on his nose. He opened the other eye. The leaf was still there. “A very small roof,” he announced to his mother. High in the rainforest canopy, their sleeping nest rocked gently among the branches. Mother stretched her long arms, and Oli copied her, almost poking himself in the ear.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/orangutan-page-03.mp3
Oli's Morning

Breakfast was a delicious collection of ripe fruit. Oli reached for a fig, but a broad leaf above it caught his attention. Rainwater from the night before rolled down its smooth surface and dripped neatly off the pointed tip. He held the leaf over his head. “Look! No drips!” Mother smiled and showed him how to hold it without tearing it.

Oli turned the leaf over and examined its strong central rib. The water travelled along the green surface instead of soaking straight through. He tried a much smaller leaf, which protected exactly one eyebrow. Then he tried the large one again. Mother patiently watched his experiments while she finished her fruit. The rainforest was full of useful things if he remembered to look at them. A leaf could be breakfast for one creature, a hiding place for another, and, apparently, an excellent roof for an orangutan with a very wet nose.

Oli's Morning

They travelled slowly through the trees, testing branches before trusting their weight to them. Oli wanted to swing straight across a wide gap, but Mother chose a safer route through neighbouring branches. Along the way they passed a sturdy fork in a tree, surrounded by thick leaves. Oli thought it looked like a splendid little room with green walls.

Oli's Afternoon

In the afternoon, the forest grew unusually dark. Thunder grumbled far away, and the birds became quieter. Oli was investigating a delicious-looking fruit when the first enormous raindrop landed on his forehead. PLOP! He looked up indignantly. “Who threw that?” Another drop landed on his nose. Mother began moving towards a more sheltered part of the canopy.

Oli could no longer hear the little birds he had been listening to that morning. Instead, the canopy creaked and shivered around him. Water ran down his reddish fur and dripped from the ends of his long fingers. He gripped a firm branch and stayed near Mother. The gap they had looked at earlier was almost invisible behind the curtain of rain. Oli did not like the idea of crossing it now. He searched the trees for something familiar. Wasn't there a strong fork in one of the branches they had passed?

Oli's Afternoon

Oli remembered his little roof and picked a large leaf. It kept some rain off his face, although his furry elbows were soon wet. He giggled at his reflection in a puddle of water caught in a branch. His hair was sticking out in every direction. “I look like a very surprised pineapple!” Mother waited while he adjusted his leafy umbrella.

Oli's Afternoon

The wind began shaking the smaller branches. Oli noticed that the route ahead looked rather exposed. He pointed towards the sturdy fork they had passed that morning. Mother examined the branches and led him back along the safer route. Under the thick leaves they found a sheltered place to wait. Oli held his umbrella above his head, feeling very pleased with his discovery.

From their sheltered perch, Oli watched the water race down the trunks. His leaf umbrella was beginning to sag. He adjusted his grip, exactly as Mother had shown him, and the rain slid harmlessly off one side. Mother had chosen a much stronger shelter than any leaf could provide, but Oli's little invention made waiting there more comfortable. A bright green beetle crawled beneath the edge of his roof. “Another customer!” Oli whispered. He moved his hand slightly to leave room for his tiny visitor.

Oli's Evening

By evening, the storm had passed. Droplets sparkled on the leaves, and insects began their familiar chorus. Oli and Mother continued through the canopy, finding a few more fruits before settling down. He offered Mother his battered leaf. It had a hole in the middle and looked rather like a green doughnut. She accepted his gift with a gentle touch.

Oli's Evening

Mother began bending branches and arranging leafy twigs into a fresh sleeping nest. Oli helped by bringing a handful of soft leaves. Some landed in the nest; others landed on his own head. He watched carefully as Mother tucked the branches together. One day, he thought, he would be able to build a wonderful nest all by himself.

Oli tried to copy the way Mother bent a branch into the nest. His first attempt sprang back, leaving him holding only a leaf. His second stayed in place, although it was rather crooked. Mother showed him where to tuck the end, and this time his little piece of the nest held. Oli gave it a delighted pat. It felt good to make something useful instead of simply watching. The storm had taught him to look carefully, and the evening had given him a chance to practise what he had learned.

Oli's Evening

As the forest darkened, Oli snuggled beside Mother. Today he had learned that clever discoveries begin with noticing ordinary things, and that learning from someone experienced is nothing to be ashamed of. His leaf umbrella lay beside him, its work finished. Far below, the rainforest whispered and rustled. Oli closed his eyes beneath his much bigger roof of stars and leaves.
