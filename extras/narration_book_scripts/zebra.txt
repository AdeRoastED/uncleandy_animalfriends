Ziggy's Morning — 1

Ziggy was convinced that his mother wore the finest stripes in the entire savanna. One stripe near her shoulder curved like a little hook. He tried to find the same shape on his own coat, twisting around until he nearly bumped into another zebra. “Looking for your tail?” his mother asked. Ziggy grinned. “Looking for your favourite stripe!”

Ziggy's Morning — 2

The herd grazed in the cool morning air. Ziggy noticed that every zebra had a different pattern, rather like the different shapes of clouds overhead. His mother explained that young zebras learn to recognise their mothers by their appearance, smell and voice. Ziggy listened to her familiar call and decided that he could recognise it anywhere, even with his eyes shut.

Ziggy tried to make a game of recognising the other zebras. One had a stripe shaped like a bent stick, another had a pattern that reminded him of a lightning flash. But when they began walking together, the patterns seemed to slide into one another like a moving picture. His mother called him, and he recognised her voice without looking. “That's much easier!” Ziggy announced. He trotted beside her, enjoying the familiar smell of her coat. The herd was full of lovely patterns, but his mother's was still his favourite.

Ziggy's Morning — 3

A yellow butterfly fluttered past his nose. Ziggy followed it for two little steps before his mother called him back. He returned immediately and nibbled the grass beside her. The butterfly landed on a flower, opened its wings and flew away. Ziggy hoped he would see it again. There were so many interesting things to discover in the savanna!

Afternoon · page 1

Ziggy's Afternoon — 1

In the afternoon the herd moved through a patch of taller grass. Ziggy spotted another yellow butterfly dancing above the stems. He watched it zigzag left and right, then turned to tell his mother about it. But the zebra beside him had a different shoulder stripe. He looked around. Suddenly all those black and white patterns seemed to be moving at once.

The butterfly seemed to have vanished into the grass. Ziggy turned one way and then another. A pair of striped legs passed in front of him, and another pair moved behind. He could smell the herd and hear the soft sounds of grazing, but the face he wanted was not immediately visible. For a moment he felt very small indeed. He wanted to dart between the adults, searching every stripe. Then he remembered how clearly he had heard his mother in the morning. Perhaps his ears could help where his eyes were finding things difficult.

Ziggy's Afternoon — 2

Ziggy felt a little flutter inside his tummy, and it wasn't a butterfly. He remembered his mother's voice. Instead of running through the herd, he stopped beside the nearby zebras and called out. He listened carefully. A familiar answer came from just beyond two grazing adults. Then he saw a shoulder with a little hooked stripe moving towards him.

Ziggy's Afternoon — 3

His mother reached him and touched her nose to his face. Ziggy breathed out a great sigh. He told her about the butterfly and how the stripes had become confusing. She stayed close as they continued through the grass. This time Ziggy watched the butterfly only while it was near them. When it disappeared, he let it go.

His mother gave him another familiar call, and Ziggy pressed his nose against her shoulder. The hooked stripe was right there, as wonderful as ever. He noticed that his heart was beating much more quickly than usual. His mother waited until he had calmed down before moving with the herd again. When the yellow butterfly returned, Ziggy pointed his nose towards it and called her attention to it. They watched it together. This time he was not the only one who got to enjoy the discovery.

Ziggy's Evening — 1

Later the herd reached a shorter grassy area. Ziggy found a lovely flower and called his mother to look. A butterfly landed on it, and they watched together without moving away from the group. Its wings opened and closed like two tiny doors. Ziggy whispered that it must be getting ready for bed. His mother gave a soft snort.

Ziggy's Evening — 2

As the sun sank, the zebras gathered together. Ziggy looked at the stripes of the other foals and imagined a grand parade, each wearing a completely different costume. He no longer thought his curiosity was a bad thing. It had helped him discover butterflies and flowers. He simply needed to remember where his family was while he explored.

A young zebra nearby had a little stripe like a crooked river. Ziggy pointed it out to his mother and then showed the foal the hooked stripe he loved so much. Soon the two youngsters were inventing names for the shapes they could see while staying near their families. “That one is a very wiggly banana!” Ziggy giggled. The evening grass smelled sweet, and the herd moved calmly together. Curiosity still felt like a wonderful thing, especially when there was somebody close by with whom he could share it.

Ziggy's Evening — 3

Under the first stars, Ziggy rested close to his mother. He could see the little hooked stripe even in the fading light. Tomorrow he would find new things to admire, and perhaps he could show them to his family instead of wandering after them alone. He closed his eyes, listening to familiar voices and the soft rustle of grass. His favourite stripe was right beside him.
