Hazel's Morning — 1

Hazel was running a very unusual shop. It had no doors, no shelves and absolutely no customers. “One acorn for later, two acorns for later,” she sang, digging little holes in the soil. As a squirrel, she hid food in many places so she could find it when the weather turned colder.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/squirrel-page-03.mp3
Hazel's Morning — 2

Beside a heart-shaped stone, she buried a particularly round acorn. “That's my best one,” she declared, patting the soil flat. Then she scampered up an oak tree, using her bushy tail to help her balance along the branches. A gust of wind shook loose a shower of leaves around her.

Hazel stopped on a branch to admire her work from above. The heart-shaped stone was clearly visible between two roots, and she was quite sure she would never confuse it with another stone. She had hidden nuts in other places too, sniffing the ground and patting the soil after each little excavation. A squirrel's memory and sense of smell can both help it find buried food. Hazel took great pride in her collection. If only her pantry had been fitted with tiny wooden signs, she thought, everything would have been much easier.

Morning · page 3

Hazel's Morning — 3

Her neighbour, a young squirrel named Nutmeg, watched Hazel hopping from branch to branch. “Do you remember every hiding place?” he asked. “Of course!” Hazel replied, rather too confidently. Then she looked down at the forest floor. There were quite a lot of stones, roots and little holes. Perhaps remembering everything would be harder than she thought.

Afternoon · page 1

Hazel's Afternoon — 1

In the afternoon, a rain shower sent leaves tumbling across the ground. Hazel sheltered in the branches until the rain passed. When she came down, her carefully arranged little pantry looked entirely different. She searched for the heart-shaped stone, brushed away wet leaves and dug where she thought her best acorn was hiding.

The heart-shaped stone had become a nearly invisible lump beneath a blanket of wet leaves. Hazel brushed aside one leaf, then three more, until her paws were muddy. A stone appeared, but its shape looked more like a potato than a heart. She stared at it suspiciously. Had the rain moved her stone? Had she simply remembered the wrong root? She felt a little cross with herself. Her wonderful morning pantry no longer seemed quite so wonderfully organised. Somewhere nearby, Nutmeg was making his own small, careful searches.

Hazel's Afternoon — 2

She found an acorn, but it was smaller and pointier. Hazel tried another spot and found nothing at all. “My beautiful acorn has disappeared!” she cried. Nutmeg was collecting food nearby. He suggested looking around the oak instead of digging up every patch of soil in the forest. Hazel agreed to try.

Afternoon · page 3

Hazel's Afternoon — 3

Beneath the tree, she found several fresh acorns. She ate one and hid another in a different place. The missing one still bothered her, but she noticed a tiny oak seedling growing near an older hiding spot. Nutmeg explained that some forgotten nuts could germinate if the conditions were right.

Hazel crouched beside the tiny oak seedling. Two little leaves spread above the ground, looking wonderfully fresh after the rain. She tried to imagine an acorn sending its first root down into the soil. Some nuts would be found and eaten, of course, and others might never grow. But a few could become trees if they found suitable conditions. Hazel looked up at the great oak above her. It was hard to believe that something so enormous had once been so small. Her missing acorn did not seem quite so much like a disaster anymore.

Evening · page 1

Hazel's Evening — 1

As the evening light softened, Hazel looked again at the seedling. Perhaps her missing acorn might also become a little tree someday, if it escaped being eaten and had enough water and sunlight. She imagined a future oak with branches where young squirrels could play. Suddenly her mistake seemed a little less terrible.

Evening · page 2

Hazel's Evening — 2

Hazel collected one more fallen acorn before gathering her bedding. She hid it carefully in a different spot, then sniffed the ground around it. She still needed food for the colder days ahead, and she was not going to stop remembering her stores just because one had gone missing. But she did not feel quite so worried about being perfect. The little seedling stood beneath the tree, its leaves catching the last light. She looked towards her nest, carrying the thought of a future forest with her.

She made one last trip to collect soft leaves for her nest, carrying them carefully up the trunk. Nutmeg waved from another branch. “Did you find your treasure?” he called. “Not that one,” Hazel replied, “but I think I found a different sort of treasure.” She smiled at the little green seedling below.

Hazel's Evening — 3

Hazel settled into her nest of twigs and leaves, wrapped her bushy tail around herself and listened to droplets falling from the branches. Not everything had gone according to her plan, and that was all right. Sometimes a small mistake could lead to a lovely surprise. She fell asleep dreaming of an entire forest of future oak trees.
