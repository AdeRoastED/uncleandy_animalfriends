Inky's Morning — 1

Inky had eight arms and, according to Inky, at least nine excellent ideas before breakfast. Today his first idea was to find a new home. His old rocky den was comfortable, but a curious crab kept visiting the entrance and rearranging his shells. “I liked that shell exactly where it was!” Inky protested. The crab seemed unimpressed.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/octopus-page-03.mp3
Inky's Morning — 2

He slipped across the seabed, changing his colour to match the sand and stones. Under a broad rock he found a wonderful hollow. It had a front entrance and a narrow opening at the back. Inky squeezed through the little gap, using his soft body to fit where a much larger fish could not follow.

Inky inspected his new home with great seriousness. Four arms explored the front entrance, two examined the floor, and the remaining two seemed to have forgotten their instructions entirely. He pulled a small empty shell aside and tested the narrow gap once again. Because an octopus has no rigid skeleton like a fish's, his soft body can pass through surprisingly small openings, provided the hard beak fits. He decided the little doorway was not terribly elegant, but it might be useful. His eight arms approved, in eight slightly different directions.

Morning · page 3

Inky's Morning — 3

He collected a few loose shells and placed them near the front entrance. Then he explored the back opening again. It led into a patch of seaweed that swayed gently in the current. “Two doorways!” he said happily. “One for arriving, one for leaving, and neither needs a doorbell.” He went out to search for breakfast.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/octopus-page-05.mp3
Inky's Afternoon — 1

In the afternoon, Inky was examining a crevice for small crabs when a large hungry fish appeared beyond the rocks. Inky flattened himself against a stone and changed his skin to blend with it. The fish swam past, then turned around. Inky stayed perfectly still. Even his most adventurous arm understood that this was no time for exploring.

The shadow of the fish passed over Inky's rock. He could see its fins moving in the blue water above him. For a moment the octopus considered making a dash for his front entrance, but the fish was between him and that part of the rock. Inky's skin matched the surrounding stone so closely that even the little pale speckles were difficult to distinguish. Still, hiding forever was not a particularly exciting plan. He needed to remember the shape of his den and the other way in.

Inky's Afternoon — 2

When the fish moved away, Inky slipped towards his new den. But another fish was investigating the front entrance, attracted by the movement of a little crab nearby. Inky stopped behind a rock. He could not safely dash through the front door. For a moment he wished he had stayed in his old home, troublesome neighbour and all.

Afternoon · page 3

Inky's Afternoon — 3

Then he remembered the narrow opening behind the den. He travelled around the rock through the seaweed, keeping close to the bottom. One arm found the gap, then another, and soon his whole soft body slipped inside. The fish outside continued searching without noticing him. Inky tucked himself into the hollow and waited quietly.

Inside the hollow, the water was dim and wonderfully peaceful. Inky peeked out just far enough to watch the shadow of the fish pass again, then withdrew into safety. His eight arms settled one by one around the rock. Outside, a little cloud of sand drifted away, and the fish finally turned towards another part of the reef. Inky waited a little longer before venturing out. “No need to open the door just because the visitor has gone,” he murmured. The reef would still be there when he was ready.

Inky's Evening — 1

By evening both fish had gone. Inky peeped out, checked the water and ventured outside. The little crab from earlier was still hiding beneath a shell. Inky left it alone and found food elsewhere. He had already had quite enough excitement for one day, and the seabed offered plenty of other places to explore.

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/octopus-page-09.mp3
Inky's Evening — 2

Back at his den, he admired the two entrances. He had not needed to be faster than every fish in the sea. He had needed to know his surroundings and choose wisely. “Nine excellent ideas,” he murmured, “but remembering one good idea is sometimes quite enough.” A tiny shrimp passed by as if agreeing with him.

Inky rearranged two empty shells near the entrance and admired his work. He was not building a grand castle, just making himself a comfortable little home. The narrow doorway, which had seemed like the least interesting part of his morning discovery, was now his favourite feature. A small crab crept past the rocks, and Inky's eyes followed it curiously. He had plenty of time for another little exploration before resting. For now, he was rather pleased to sit quietly in the place he knew so well.

Inky's Evening — 3

The water darkened and the seaweed rocked gently outside his back doorway. Inky arranged his shells, curled his arms comfortably and rested. His skin softened into the colours of the rock around him. Somewhere outside, the ocean carried on with its busy evening. Inside the little den, all eight arms were finally ready for a quiet moment.
