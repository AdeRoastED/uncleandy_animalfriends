Zuri's Morning — 1

Zuri woke with a leaf tickling her nose. She stretched her long neck and plucked it from the branch above her. Breakfast had practically delivered itself! Beside her stood a younger giraffe named Lolo, stretching so hard that his ears wobbled. The leaf he wanted remained just beyond his reach. “I think my neck forgot to grow last night,” he complained.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/giraffe-page-03.mp3
Zuri's Morning — 2

Zuri showed Lolo a lower branch with plenty of tender leaves. She curled her long tongue carefully around a leafy twig, avoiding the sharp thorns. Lolo copied her and chewed happily. From her higher position, Zuri noticed a row of trees stretching towards a sheltered clearing. She remembered the shape of the tallest tree, which looked rather like an open umbrella.

A small bird landed high in the umbrella tree and looked down at them. Lolo lifted his head until his ears pointed almost straight up. “Can you tell what it is doing?” he asked. Zuri could see it hopping between the branches, but she could not see the tiny insect Lolo had noticed beside his feet. They laughed. The tall view and the short view each had something interesting to offer. Zuri looked back at the shape of the umbrella tree before they wandered on. It stood out beautifully against the morning sky.

Morning · page 3

Zuri's Morning — 3

The giraffes wandered slowly across the savanna, browsing as they went. Lolo asked whether Zuri could see all the way to the moon. “Only if the moon comes down for breakfast,” she joked. Their mothers were nearby, watching the surroundings. Zuri enjoyed being tall, but she sometimes forgot that the world looked quite different to someone much smaller.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/giraffe-page-05.mp3
Zuri's Afternoon — 1

After midday a strong wind swept across the plain. Dry leaves rattled and dust rose from the ground. Lolo blinked and tucked his head close to his mother's side. The giraffes began moving away from the exposed grassland. Zuri could see the tops of the trees she had noticed earlier, although the dust hid much of the ground.

The dust made the familiar savanna seem as though somebody had rubbed an enormous brown pencil across it. Zuri could still see over some of the drifting dust, but when she lowered her head she understood why Lolo looked worried. The grass, the trees and even the giraffes' legs seemed to disappear and reappear. Lolo kept close to his mother. Zuri wanted to tell him that everything was perfectly fine, but she could see how uncomfortable the wind had become. Instead, she looked for the landmark she remembered.

Zuri's Afternoon — 2

There was the umbrella-shaped tree! Zuri stayed beside her mother as the adults moved towards the sheltered area. Lolo tried to hurry, but his long young legs seemed to have different ideas about which direction to go. Zuri slowed down beside him. “We aren't racing the wind,” she said. “It would probably cheat anyway.”

Afternoon · page 3

Zuri's Afternoon — 3

As they reached the trees, the wind became less fierce. Lolo was hungry again, but the first branches he found were too high. Zuri spotted a smaller tree a little farther along. She waited for the adults to move towards it, then showed Lolo the leaves within his reach. He took a bite and smiled. The dusty afternoon suddenly seemed much less troublesome.

The little clearing was not completely still, but the trees broke the worst of the wind. Zuri blinked the last of the dust from her eyes and watched Lolo pull a leaf from a low branch. His long, dark tongue curled around it rather neatly. “My neck did grow a little, after all!” he said proudly. Zuri laughed and chose a higher branch for herself. Neither had to struggle for the same leaf. Above them, the umbrella tree swayed like a very large parasol, and the afternoon began to feel comfortable again.

Evening · page 1

Zuri's Evening — 1

By evening the wind had softened and the sky was turning gold. Zuri watched Lolo discover a particularly tasty cluster of leaves. He called her over, proud of finding something himself. The leaves were low enough that Zuri had to bend her neck to reach them. “Your turn to stretch down!” he giggled. Zuri happily shared his discovery.

Evening · page 2

Zuri's Evening — 2

Their mothers continued browsing nearby. Zuri noticed that giraffes did not all need to eat from the same height. Young ones could reach different branches, and older ones could reach the taller trees.

Lolo found one more small cluster hidden among the lower twigs and called Zuri over. She bent down to see what he meant. From his height, the leaves were obvious; from hers, a broad branch had hidden them. “I would have walked straight past those,” she admitted. Lolo looked as pleased as if he had discovered an entirely new tree. Zuri decided that being tall did not make her the best discoverer of absolutely everything. She had a useful view, and Lolo had a useful view. Together they could notice far more.

She thought about the clearing and the little tree. Being able to see far was useful, but noticing what someone else needed was even more important.

Zuri's Evening — 3

As darkness approached, Lolo settled near his mother while Zuri stood quietly beside her own. The umbrella-shaped tree was now a dark silhouette against the sky. Zuri wondered what tomorrow might bring and what Lolo might discover before she did. Perhaps they would find another wonderful breakfast together. The breeze whispered through the leaves, and the savanna grew peaceful.
