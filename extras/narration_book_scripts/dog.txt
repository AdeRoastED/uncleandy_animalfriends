Bruno's Morning

Bruno woke to a wonderful smell drifting through the kitchen. Toast! Unfortunately, the toast belonged to his human. Bruno had his own breakfast, which he ate with the enthusiasm of a dog who had not eaten for approximately a hundred years. In reality, it had been since yesterday evening. After breakfast he trotted outside, ready for his morning inspection.

Morning · page 2

Bruno's Morning

At the garden gate he discovered a glove lying beside a patch of mint. Bruno sniffed the glove, the mint and a rather uninteresting stone. Dogs can gather an extraordinary amount of information through their noses. This glove smelled familiar. He carried it back to his human, who laughed and thanked him. Bruno wagged proudly. Another mystery solved!

Bruno conducted one final inspection of the garden before declaring it ready for the day. There was the mint, the patch of damp earth beneath the tap, and the very important corner where a squirrel sometimes appeared. Each place had its own story of smells. He heard his human laugh behind him. “What a busy nose!” Bruno lifted his head proudly. If somebody ever needed a garden detective, he was quite certain that he would be the first dog they called.

Bruno's Morning

A little puppy called Pip was visiting that day. Pip chased a butterfly while Bruno investigated a strange rattling sound. Every time the wind blew, the garden gate shook. Bruno tilted his head. The latch looked different from usual. His human was nearby, hanging washing, and Bruno went over to sit beside her. Then Pip dropped a yellow ball at his feet. Inspection time was over, apparently.

Afternoon · page 1

Bruno's Afternoon

By afternoon, the wind was blowing harder. The washing danced on the line, and Pip bounced after the yellow ball. Bruno lay beneath a tree, enjoying the shade. Suddenly there came a familiar sound. Rattle, rattle, click! The gate had opened a little. Beyond it was a pavement where people and bicycles sometimes passed. Pip had spotted another butterfly.

The gap at the gate was only a little wider than Pip's head, but the puppy was very small. Bruno could hear the thin whir of bicycle tyres beyond the fence. His tail stopped wagging. The butterfly was doing what butterflies always did, wandering wherever it liked, and Pip was following it without noticing anything else. The garden suddenly seemed much too quiet. Bruno looked towards the house. His human was busy inside. How could he get her attention and persuade Pip to turn around at the same time?

Bruno's Afternoon

The yellow ball! Bruno remembered how much Pip loved it. He picked it up and trotted away from the gate, towards the house. Then he dropped it and nudged it across the grass. Pip heard the familiar bounce and spun around. Suddenly the butterfly was forgotten. The puppy bounded after the ball, tumbling over his own enormous paws. Bruno barked again to attract his human's attention.

Bruno kept the ball near the house, far from the opening. He rolled it once more, and Pip pounced so enthusiastically that the ball bounced under his chin. Bruno gave another sharp bark towards the kitchen. This time he heard footsteps. He stood between the puppy and the gate without trying to chase either the butterfly or the bicycles. Pip, delighted by the game, dropped the ball at Bruno's feet. “You found me!” his expression seemed to say. Bruno gave him a friendly sniff. Yes, he certainly had.

Bruno's Evening

Their human hurried over and secured the gate. She checked the latch carefully and moved the dogs inside while she arranged to have it repaired. Bruno received a gentle pat. Pip received a drink of water and a quiet place to rest. He had no idea why everyone seemed so relieved. He was simply delighted that his favourite ball had returned.

Evening · page 2

Bruno's Evening

Later, with the gate safely fixed, the dogs enjoyed a supervised game in the garden. Pip tried to carry two toys at once, which was rather difficult with such a small mouth. Bruno watched him drop one, pick it up and drop the other. Even a great detective could not solve that particular problem. He settled beside his human, feeling pleased with the day's work.

When the gate was closed again, Bruno gave it a very serious inspection from a safe distance. No rattle, no click. Their human tested the latch herself and thanked Bruno for making such a fuss. He was rewarded with a scratch behind the ears, exactly where he liked it best. Pip settled down with the yellow ball and began licking it as though it were the finest dinner in the world. Bruno thought about the butterfly. He had not caught it. He had not even left the garden. Yet his little adventure had ended rather well.

Bruno's Evening

As evening arrived, Pip curled up beside Bruno. The older dog had discovered that courage did not always mean rushing into danger. Sometimes it meant staying safe, thinking carefully and calling someone who could help. Outside, the wind had become a gentle whisper. Inside, two sleepy tails lay still, and two happy dogs dreamed of a garden full of yellow balls.
