Kiki's Morning — 1

Kiki woke to a chorus of birds in the rainforest canopy. She stretched her wings and admired her enormous colourful bill. “Good morning, breakfast!” she called to a cluster of ripe fruit. Her brother fluttered onto the branch beside her. Kiki's bill looked heavy, but its lightweight structure helped her reach fruit without carrying a great weight on her head.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/toucan-page-03.mp3
Kiki's Morning — 2

The two young toucans hopped along the branches, choosing ripe berries. Kiki tossed one into the air and caught it neatly. Her brother tried the same trick and missed. The berry bounced off a leaf and disappeared. “Perhaps the forest was hungry too,” Kiki giggled. They continued until they reached a tree with a broad crown of thick, overlapping leaves.

From beneath the broad leaves, the canopy looked like a green ceiling. Kiki could hear water dripping from a plant high above them, but the branch under her feet was almost dry. Her brother hopped to one side and found a little sheltered perch. Their large bills helped them reach fruit that was difficult to get at from a branch, but even the finest bill could not make a flimsy twig stronger. Kiki remembered where the sturdy branches were. She was very interested in a nearby tree that still held several ripe figs.

Kiki's Morning — 3

Under those leaves grew a cluster of fruit that was not quite ripe. Kiki wanted to try one, but her brother suggested leaving it for another day. Nearby, they spotted a different branch with ripe figs tucked beneath the leafy roof. Kiki remembered the place. From there, she could see their family's tree hollow across a small gap in the canopy.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/toucan-page-05.mp3
Kiki's Afternoon — 1

After midday, clouds gathered above the rainforest. The wind shook the branches, and raindrops began tapping the leaves. Kiki and her brother moved closer to the trunk of a sturdy tree. The rain grew louder until it sounded like a thousand tiny drums. Kiki tried calling over the noise, but her brother could barely hear her.

A particularly loud gust made the leaves turn their pale undersides towards the sky. Kiki held the branch and tucked herself close to the trunk. She had expected the shower to pass in a moment, but the drops kept coming. Her brother's stomach made a little sound. “Mine too,” she admitted. Their favourite feeding branch was somewhere beyond the wet leaves. She wondered whether the fruit there would still be waiting when the rain stopped. Then she remembered the broad crown they had visited earlier and the protected branches beneath it.

Kiki's Afternoon — 2

When the shower eased, they flew a short distance to their usual feeding branch. Several ripe fruits had fallen during the storm. Her brother looked disappointed. Kiki remembered the sheltered figs from the morning. She waited until the wind had quietened, then called to him and flew towards the familiar broad tree. The leaves were dripping, but the branch beneath them was still full of fruit.

Afternoon · page 3

Kiki's Afternoon — 3

Kiki reached for the largest fig. Then she noticed her brother struggling to reach one on a thin twig. Instead of eating immediately, she hopped aside and let him use the sturdy branch. He caught a fig and gave a delighted croak. They took turns reaching for the fruit. There was plenty for both, and neither needed to risk balancing on the slippery twig.

The fig was soft and sweet, and Kiki ate it with a little toss of her head. Her brother had found one of his own. From their sturdy perch they could reach another cluster without leaning out too far, so they moved along the branch together. A few seeds fell into the forest below as they ate. Somewhere, much later, a seed might find a place to grow. Kiki was not thinking that far ahead, however. She was thinking that a shared picnic was a great improvement on two very wet, very hungry toucans.

Evening · page 1

Kiki's Evening — 1

By evening the clouds had drifted away. Drops of water sparkled in the golden light. Kiki and her brother flew back towards their family's hollow, stopping to eat a few more berries along the way. Like many fruit-eating birds, toucans help forests grow by carrying and spreading seeds. Kiki had no idea that her breakfast might help a new tree grow somewhere far below.

Evening · page 2

Kiki's Evening — 2

At their resting tree, Kiki tucked her bill among her feathers. Her brother made one last attempt at tossing a berry and caught it perfectly. Kiki gave a cheerful little call. “Tomorrow you'll be teaching me!” she said. The two settled near their family as the forest grew quieter, listening to the last drops falling from the leaves.

The forest seemed to be drying itself one leaf at a time. A little bird called from a nearby branch, and Kiki answered with a soft croak. Her brother was still pleased with his successful berry toss. He offered her the next one, and she accepted it without trying to show him how much better she could catch it. They had each noticed something useful that day. Kiki imagined all the places they might discover together tomorrow, and then tucked her head into a comfortable position. There would be no need to rush breakfast.

Kiki's Evening — 3

Kiki remembered the fruit she had wanted to eat all by herself. Sharing the branch had made the afternoon much happier, and remembering a little detail had helped them both. She decided that tomorrow she would look for more wonderful places to explore together. Beneath the shelter of their tree, the young toucans grew sleepy. Outside, the rainforest shimmered under the evening sky.
