Ella's Morning — 1

The sun was barely above the grass when Ella woke to a very peculiar noise. Splosh! Her little cousin had tried to drink with his trunk and sprayed his own forehead instead. Ella laughed and showed him how to draw water into his trunk and pour it into his mouth. He tried again. This time he watered Ella's toes.

Morning · page 2

Ella's Morning — 2

Grandmother led the herd away from the muddy pool towards breakfast. Beside the path stood a tall termite mound with a crooked top. “A birthday cake!” Ella announced. Grandmother explained that tiny termites had built it, and that the mound was a useful landmark on the way to another waterhole. Ella looked back twice, trying to remember its funny shape.

Ella tried to imagine the little termites building such an enormous mound. She could hardly see the tiny creatures, yet their home rose above the grass like a tower. Grandmother pointed out another landmark, a pair of trees with branches bent in different directions. Ella made up a little song: “Crooked cake, two funny trees, rocky hill and water, please!” Her cousin joined in at the wrong moment and made an enthusiastic trumpet. Grandmother waited patiently until they were ready to continue. Ella had no idea how useful her silly song might become.

Ella's Morning — 3

The elephants pulled up grasses and reached for leaves. Ella used the little finger-like tips of her trunk to pick up a particularly delicious stem. Her cousin tried to copy her, but grabbed a dry twig instead. As they ate, Grandmother pointed out a distant rocky ridge. Ella wondered why grown-ups spent so much time looking at things that weren't lunch.

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/elephant-page-05.mp3
Ella's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon the sun was hot enough to make Ella flap her enormous ears. Moving air over their ears helps elephants cool down. Her cousin was no longer bouncing along beside her. His steps had become slow, and his mother stopped to let him rest in the shade. The little pool they had passed earlier was now far behind them.

The herd moved slowly, their feet raising little clouds of dust. Ella noticed that the older elephants were no longer pulling at every patch of grass. They were looking ahead, searching for the familiar routes. Her cousin leaned into his mother's side and gave a tired little sound. Ella remembered his cheerful morning shower, and suddenly wished she could turn the whole sky into a waterhole. But she was still a young elephant. She could not make rain, and she did not know the way by herself. What could she remember that might help?

Ella's Afternoon — 2

Ella wanted to rush ahead and find water herself. Then she noticed something poking above the grass: a crooked brown tower. Could it be the birthday cake? She moved closer to her grandmother and gave a low rumble. Elephants can communicate with deep sounds, some too low for human ears to hear. Grandmother turned towards her.

Afternoon · page 3

Ella's Afternoon — 3

“Look, the termite mound!” Ella said. Grandmother recognised it immediately. She knew the route from there to a dependable waterhole beyond the ridge. The herd set off together at a comfortable pace. Ella walked near her cousin and his mother. Whenever the little one stopped, Ella stopped too. Finding water would be no good if they left someone behind.

Beyond the mound, Ella saw the two funny trees from her song. Farther away, the ridge rose above the golden grass. Grandmother had travelled this way many times, and she chose the familiar path rather than taking a shortcut across an unfamiliar patch of land. Ella matched the rhythm of the herd's footsteps. From somewhere beyond the ridge came a faint call of birds. Her cousin lifted his head. Ella hoped it meant water was close, but she waited for Grandmother to lead the way.

Ella's Evening — 1

At last Ella smelled damp earth. Beyond the ridge lay a wide pool, shining in the late sunlight. The grown-ups checked the surroundings before the calves approached. Ella drank deeply, then splashed water over her back. Her cousin attempted another enormous spray. This time it landed right on Grandmother's dusty knee. Even Grandmother seemed amused.

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/elephant-page-09.mp3
Ella's Evening — 2

As the air cooled, the elephants dusted their damp skin with loose earth. The coating helped protect them from the sun and insects. Ella asked Grandmother how she remembered so many places. “One journey at a time,” Grandmother replied. Ella looked towards the ridge and imagined all the paths she might learn as she grew older.

Ella tried her little song again. This time she added the pool and the place where they had found shade for her cousin. Grandmother listened, then reminded her that the same landscape could look different after rain or during another season. Remembering a shape was helpful, but experience taught elephants which places they could depend on and when. Ella leaned her head gently against Grandmother's side. She was glad she had asked for help instead of running ahead. There were many more journeys to learn, and she had an excellent teacher beside her.

Ella's Evening — 3

When the stars appeared, the herd gathered quietly, with the little calf close to his mother. Ella felt proud, not because she had known everything, but because she had noticed something useful and asked someone who knew more. Her cousin leaned against her side. Tomorrow there would be more paths, more grasses and probably more accidental showers. Tonight, everyone had enough water, and Ella was happy.
