Fin's Morning — 1

Fin loved mornings because the sea was full of voices. His dolphin pod clicked and whistled as they travelled together. Fin answered with his own familiar whistle, then leapt above the waves. Splash! He landed beside his aunt, who gave him a look that said she had not requested a shower. Fin was delighted anyway.

Morning · page 2

Fin's Morning — 2

His aunt suggested a listening game. She sent clicks through the water and listened to the echoes returning from nearby objects. Fin tried it too. A rocky outcrop returned a different echo from a small fish. He could learn about things even when the water was too cloudy to see them clearly. It felt like a wonderful secret.

Fin tried clicking towards a patch of bubbles and listening to the returning sound. There was so much happening underwater that he sometimes had trouble deciding what to listen to first. His aunt made a whistle, and Fin turned towards her. A click could help him investigate his surroundings, while a familiar whistle could tell him something about his companions. Fin liked the idea that the ocean contained messages even where his eyes could see very little. He was determined to become an excellent listener, although for now he was mostly excellent at making noise.

Fin's Morning — 3

Near a bend in the coast, Fin noticed how the pod's whistles carried through the water. His aunt reminded him to stay close and listen for familiar calls. Fin practised answering instead of making his loudest possible noise. “But my loudest noise is magnificent!” he said. His aunt smiled. “So is knowing when to be quiet.”

Fin's Afternoon — 1

After lunch, the pod travelled through water stirred cloudy by the waves. Fin could barely see the dolphins beside him. A younger calf named Pip became nervous when his mother's shape disappeared in the murk. He whistled repeatedly, then began swimming in the wrong direction. Fin heard him and called to his aunt, who was swimming nearby.

Pip's whistle came again, shorter and more worried than before. Fin wanted to swim directly towards the sound, but the cloudy water made it difficult to see exactly where the little dolphin was heading. His aunt stayed close and gave a reassuring call. Fin listened for a reply. Somewhere beyond the murk was another familiar whistle, faint but clear enough to recognise. The morning game suddenly seemed very important. Could Fin help Pip listen for his mother's voice instead of swimming faster and faster in the wrong direction?

Fin's Afternoon — 2

The aunt slowed down and gave a familiar whistle. Fin wanted to leap and splash to show Pip where they were, but remembered their morning game. Splashing would not make the water clearer. He stayed close to his aunt and listened. Soon they heard Pip's mother calling from the other side of the cloudy patch.

Afternoon · page 3

Fin's Afternoon — 3

Fin's aunt guided the little group towards the mother's call. Fin kept answering gently so Pip could follow alongside them. As the water cleared, the calf spotted his mother and hurried to her side. She greeted him with a whistle and a gentle touch. Fin felt so happy that he almost forgot to breathe, then surfaced with an enormous puff.

Pip stayed close to his mother for a while, occasionally looking towards Fin and his aunt. The water was clearing now, and sunlight made long, wavering lines beneath the surface. Fin gave a small whistle, and Pip answered with one of his own. This time it sounded much more cheerful. They surfaced together, taking comfortable breaths before diving again. Fin felt rather proud, but the best part was not being the loudest dolphin in the sea. It was hearing the little calf's voice change from frightened to happy.

Fin's Evening — 1

In the evening, the pod gathered in quieter water. Pip swam close to his mother while Fin told his aunt how worried he had been. “You listened before you acted,” she said. “That helped us find him.” Fin realised that his favourite trick, making a spectacular splash, had not been the right trick for every situation.

Evening · page 2

Fin's Evening — 2

He practised the listening game once more. This time he waited for the returning echoes and the calls of his companions instead of rushing ahead. Pip joined in, making little clicks of his own. Fin imagined the sea as a great conversation, with room for every voice, even the smallest and quietest ones.

The dolphins moved through a brighter stretch of water. Fin heard a familiar whistle and waited before answering, giving the others a chance to call as well. Pip swam comfortably near his mother, practising the same listening game. A school of small fish flashed beneath them like scattered pieces of silver. Fin turned to watch, then remembered to stay with the pod. He had discovered that the sea was full of wonderful things to hear, and that listening to a friend could be just as interesting as discovering something new.

Fin's Evening — 3

Under the fading sky, the dolphins travelled slowly together. Dolphins can rest part of their brain while remaining able to breathe, and Fin began to settle into the gentle rhythm of the pod. He heard his aunt's familiar whistle nearby. Tomorrow he would leap again. Tonight, listening to the voices he loved was adventure enough.
