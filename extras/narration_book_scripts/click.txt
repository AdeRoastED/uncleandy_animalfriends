Click's Morning

“To-kay! To-kay!” Click gave one last call before the sun climbed over the garden wall. The spotted gecko had spent the night hunting insects, and now his favourite wooden beam was waiting for him. He climbed upside down without a wobble. Tiny structures on his toe pads helped him grip the surface. “Who needs a staircase?” he whispered, squeezing into his shady hiding place.

Click's Morning

Tap, tap, tap! Click opened one eye. A long leaf outside the window was brushing the glass whenever the breeze blew. He watched it carefully. When the breeze stopped, the tapping stopped too. “A leaf with a very important announcement,” he decided.

Before settling back to sleep, Click inspected the leaf from his beam. It was green on one side, pale underneath, and it turned over like a little door every time the wind came through. He counted the taps on the glass. Two, then four, then nothing at all. “That is a very strange sort of conversation,” he thought. Unlike a gecko's call, the tapping always came from exactly the same spot. He remembered that detail before his eyelids grew too heavy to keep open.

He listened for a little longer, then tucked his tail comfortably beside him.

Click's Morning

A younger gecko named Dot lived behind a nearby beam. Dot was still learning the sounds of the garden and had a habit of answering everything, even the squeaky door. Click heard a tiny chirp from Dot's hiding place. “Time for sleep,” he murmured. Tokay geckos are mostly active at night, so both neighbours disappeared into the cool shadows as the morning grew brighter.

Click's Afternoon

In the afternoon, rain drummed on the roof. Click woke when a particularly large drop struck the window. Tap, tap! Dot chirped from behind the other beam. Tap, tap! Dot chirped again. Click blinked. Was Dot having a conversation with the leaf? He peeked out just far enough to see the wet garden shining below.

Dot edged towards the end of the beam, peering into the dripping garden. “Perhaps the other gecko has lost its way,” came the little voice. Click could understand the worry. Once, when he was much smaller, the sound of a broom brushing the wall had made him hide for an entire evening. There was nothing silly about a sound you didn't understand. He watched Dot's tiny toes grip the wood and decided to turn the mysterious tapping into a game.

Click's Afternoon

“Hello? Are you there?” Dot called in their imaginary gecko language. The leaf answered with another tap. Click almost laughed, but then remembered how strange the garden had seemed when he was young. He did not want to embarrass his neighbour. “Listen for a moment,” he called softly. A gust rattled the leaf three times, then everything went quiet.

Click's Afternoon

Dot waited. No answer came until the next gust. “That's funny,” Dot whispered. Click explained what he had seen in the morning. “The wind is moving a leaf. It isn't another gecko.” Dot was disappointed. Click promised that the garden would have plenty of real voices once darkness fell. For now they returned to their sheltered resting places.

“Let's make a prediction,” Click suggested. “When the next gust comes, watch the leaf.” The two geckos waited. A raindrop rolled down the glass, and a wet branch bent towards the window. Tap, tap, tap! Dot's eyes widened. “It moved before the sound!” Another gust brought exactly the same result. Dot gave a small, delighted chirp. The mystery had been solved not by guessing louder, but by looking closely. Even a rather boring leaf had turned out to be interesting.

Click's Evening

At dusk, the rain stopped and insects gathered around the garden lights. Click emerged, stretched his toes and called, “To-kay! To-kay!” From a distant tree came a real answering call. Dot listened in astonishment. It sounded quite different from the tapping leaf. Then a moth fluttered past, and Click caught his supper with a quick movement.

Evening · page 2

Click's Evening

Dot gave a little chirp of excitement. “I think I know the difference now!” Click smiled. The two geckos explored their own safe corners of the wall, leaving each other plenty of space. Dot even noticed a beetle moving before Click did. “Excellent listening, neighbour!” Click said. Dot looked very pleased indeed.

A faint buzz rose from beneath the lamp, and Dot turned towards it before anything was visible. A beetle appeared on the wall, its tiny legs ticking against the plaster. Click was impressed. Dot's careful listening was already useful for hunting, and there were plenty of insects for both neighbours to explore separately. From somewhere in the darkness came another deep tokay call. Dot stopped to listen, then looked at the leaf. “You can keep tapping,” the youngster told it. “I have other things to hear now.”

Click's Evening

The moon rose above the wet garden. The leaf tapped once more, and this time Dot did not answer. Instead, the youngster gave a tiny amused chirp. Click settled into a sheltered crack for a short rest before his next nighttime hunt. He thought about how much there was to discover when you stopped and listened. Outside, the garden whispered its many different songs.
