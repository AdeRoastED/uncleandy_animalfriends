Nanu's Morning — 1

Nanu woke with a snowflake sitting on the end of his nose. He sneezed so loudly that his mother turned around. “A very impressive alarm clock,” she chuckled. He rolled onto his back and rubbed his thick fur in the snow. The Arctic morning was bright, and Nanu was eager to explore.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/polarbear-page-03.mp3
Nanu's Morning — 2

As he followed Mother, he noticed the enormous prints her paws left behind. His own broad paws were smaller but worked like snowshoes, spreading his weight over the snow. The fur beneath them helped him grip. He tried making a paw print shaped like a heart. It looked more like a squashed potato.

Nanu inspected his mother's footprints, then tried to put each of his smaller paws inside one of them. It was rather like walking along a row of enormous bowls. The broad paws helped him travel across the snowy ground, but Mother still watched carefully where she stepped. Snow could cover uneven surfaces, and conditions on the ice could change. Nanu looked up at a familiar ridge that stood out against the pale sky. He thought he could probably recognise it anywhere, although he had never seen it disappear behind a curtain of blowing snow.

Nanu's Morning — 3

Near a ridge, Mother paused to sniff the air. Nanu peered into a hollow protected from the wind. Inside, the snow was quiet and soft. “My snow castle!” he announced. Mother examined the place before they moved on. “Remember it,” she said. “A good explorer notices where to rest, not just where to go.”

Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/polarbear-page-05.mp3
Nanu's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon, clouds had covered the bright sky. Snow began to blow across their path. Nanu wanted to prove that he was a brave polar bear by racing ahead, but Mother stopped him. She listened, sniffed and looked for firm ground. Through the drifting snow, the familiar shapes had become difficult to recognise.

A gust swept across the ridge, and the outlines of the distant snowdrifts vanished. Nanu blinked, then tried to find the familiar shape again. His paws felt steady on the ground where Mother stood, but the landscape ahead was no longer clear. He had been imagining a splendid race across the white world, with his mother cheering behind him. Now he found himself wanting to stay close to her instead. Was stopping really part of being a strong polar bear? He watched her carefully, hoping to discover the answer.

Nanu's Afternoon — 2

Nanu spotted the ridge, with its little crooked top like a bear's ear. “The castle!” he called. Mother recognised it too and led him carefully towards the sheltered hollow. As soon as they stepped inside, the wind sounded much quieter. Nanu shook the snow from his ears, showering Mother with tiny white flakes.

Afternoon · page 3
Target: audio/bedtime/polarbear-page-07.mp3
Nanu's Afternoon — 3

“Are we giving up?” he asked, disappointed that their walk had stopped. “No,” Mother replied. “We're waiting for better conditions.” They rested together, and Nanu watched the snow stream over the ridge. He invented names for the shapes in the clouds until his eyelids grew heavy. Even a polar bear needed time to recover his energy.

The wind made little streams of snow curl around their sheltered place. Nanu watched one drift grow slowly against a rock. Mother rested beside him, keeping him close while conditions improved. Every now and then he lifted his nose and looked towards the ridge. The air was becoming clearer, but Mother was in no hurry. Nanu discovered that waiting did not feel quite so difficult when there were small changes to notice. At last the shape of the ridge appeared again, and he gave a very satisfied little grunt.

Nanu's Evening — 1

When the weather eased, Mother checked their surroundings again. The ridge was clear now, and the firm snowy ground stretched ahead. Nanu followed at her side instead of running. He spotted the earlier paw prints, almost filled with snow, and laughed at the little potato-shaped one. It was still recognisable.

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/polarbear-page-09.mp3
Nanu's Evening — 2

They continued until they found another comfortable place to settle. Nanu felt proud, although he hadn't raced anywhere. “I thought being brave meant never stopping,” he admitted. Mother nudged his cheek. “Sometimes the wisest and bravest thing is knowing when to stop.” He tucked that thought away like a precious little stone.

Nanu looked back at the familiar snow shapes and the footprints they had left together. Some of the older marks had already been covered by drifting snow. He was glad they had not depended on those marks alone. Mother had chosen their route when the conditions were clearer, and he had learned by following her. His paws felt pleasantly tired now, not from a great race but from an ordinary journey made carefully. He leaned against Mother's warm fur. Tomorrow's adventure could begin when tomorrow was ready.

Nanu's Evening — 3

Evening brought a gentle blue light over the snow. Nanu curled beside Mother, his nose tucked beneath a furry paw. The wind whispered far beyond their resting place. In his dreams he was the king of a tiny snow castle, with one important royal rule: every explorer was allowed to rest.
