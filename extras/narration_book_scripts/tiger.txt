Tiko's Morning — 1

Tiko woke beneath a curtain of green leaves. His mother was stretching her enormous paws, and he decided to stretch his too. Unfortunately, his back paw caught a twig. CRACK! A startled bird flew away. “I meant to do that,” Tiko announced. Mother blinked slowly. Tigers have excellent hearing, and Tiko was beginning to discover just how many secrets the forest could tell him.

Tiko's Morning — 2

They walked through tall grass where sunlight painted golden stripes across the ground. Tiko looked down at his own black stripes. When he stood still among the shadows, even his tail seemed to disappear. Mother showed him how to place his soft paws quietly. He tried three careful steps, then sneezed. A butterfly fluttered away, apparently unimpressed by his magnificent tiger skills.

A tiny patch of sunlight moved along Tiko's shoulder as the leaves swayed above him. When he shifted into the grass, his mother seemed to disappear too, even though she was only a short distance away. Her stripes broke up the outline of her body among the stems. Tiko decided to test his own disappearing trick. He crouched very still, until a ticklish blade of grass touched his nose. His magnificent hiding place ended with another sneeze. Mother looked at him, and Tiko was sure she was smiling.

Tiko's Morning — 3

Suddenly something rattled beside a fallen log. Tiko crouched and wiggled his bottom. “A mysterious forest monster!” he whispered. But Mother waited, and so did he. A little dry seedpod rolled into view, pushed by the breeze. Tiko laughed. He nudged it gently with his nose. Mother reminded him that a sound alone cannot tell you what is hiding nearby.

Tiko's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon the air was warm and heavy. Tiko followed Mother towards a shallow stream where they could cool themselves. Unlike many cats, tigers are comfortable in water. Tiko dipped one paw into the stream and splashed his own whiskers. He looked around to see whether anyone had noticed. Mother was already wading peacefully through the shallows.

The water felt lovely after the heat of the forest, and Tiko paddled gently in the shallow part near Mother. Farther along, the stream twisted around a bank covered with tall plants. A little piece of earth dropped into the water with a soft plop. Tiko watched the ripples travel away. Then a leaf slipped down the same bank. He was curious, but his mother was ready to leave, so he followed her. He did not know that the very same place would catch his attention again before the afternoon was over.

Tiko's Afternoon — 2

On their way back, another rustling sound came from the grass. Tiko almost sprang forward, but remembered his morning monster. He stopped and watched. A long branch was swaying in the breeze. Beyond it, the muddy bank had crumbled into the stream. The grass concealed the broken edge completely. His whiskers twitched. That would have been a very unpleasant surprise.

Tiko's Afternoon — 3

Mother was investigating a scent nearby. Tiko gave a soft call and waited for her. When she returned, he looked towards the broken bank. She sniffed the ground and turned towards a firmer route among the trees. Tiko followed, stepping carefully over roots. He felt rather proud that his patient little investigation had helped them avoid the slippery edge.

As they passed the firmer ground, Tiko heard the branch scraping once more. This time he could see where it moved against the edge of the bank. A little clump of earth slid away, leaving the grass leaning over the water. He looked up at Mother. The noise had not been a creature inviting him to play at all. The seedpod in the morning had been harmless; this new sound had been a useful warning. Two mysteries, two different answers. Tiko found that rather exciting.

Tiko's Evening — 1

As evening approached, the forest changed its tune. Frogs began calling, insects chirped and birds settled into their roosts. Tiko tried to identify each sound. One frog sounded remarkably like his own stomach. He decided that perhaps it was time for another meal. Mother led him deeper into the forest, where the shadows grew longer and their striped coats blended into the trees.

Tiko's Evening — 2

Later, they paused beneath a broad tree. Tiko practised his silent steps around a root. This time he managed to move without cracking a single twig. He was so delighted that he nearly announced his success at the top of his voice. Instead, he gave Mother a gentle nuzzle. She rested her chin near his shoulder, and he curled his tail around his paws.

A distant bird gave a short call, followed by a soft splash somewhere near the stream. Tiko lifted his ears, but he stayed comfortably where he was. There were hundreds of sounds in the forest, and he would never understand all of them in a single day. He decided to begin with the ones close to him: Mother's breathing, the wind in the leaves, the quiet tap of a falling seed. Before long he could hardly keep his eyes open. Listening, he discovered, could be wonderfully relaxing as well as useful.

Tiko's Evening — 3

Tiko thought about the seedpod and the hidden bank. Being quick was useful, but knowing when to wait was just as important. He didn't need to pounce on every mystery to be a wonderful tiger. For now, he could rest beside Mother before their next nighttime adventure. The forest whispered softly around them, and his golden eyes slowly closed.
