Naga's Morning — 1

In a make-believe tale inspired by the Naga traditions of the Mekong region, a gentle serpent named Naga watched the morning sunlight dance across the river. The Mekong is real, and Naga figures hold important meanings in several cultures. Our particular Naga was an imaginary character who loved the colours of flowers and water.

Morning · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/naga-page-03.mp3
Naga's Morning — 2

The village in our story was preparing a little festival beside the river. Its imaginary helpers had made magical floating flowers for an evening display. Naga noticed several gathering in a sheltered inlet near a bend, where the water moved gently. A beautiful purple blossom spun slowly in a circle.

Naga watched the purple blossom turn beneath the willow-like branches near the bank. In this imaginary celebration, the flowers were enchanted decorations that the village helpers would collect again rather than leave in the river. The quiet inlet held the blossom while the main current moved swiftly past. Naga admired the different colours reflected on the water and remembered the shape of the sheltered bend. Mali was already busy preparing the festival, but the little flower had given both friends a reason to notice a place they might otherwise have passed.

Naga's Morning — 3

A young festival sprite named Mali waved from the bank. “That is my favourite flower!” she called. Naga guided the magical blossom towards the sheltered edge. Afternoon · page 1
Target: audio/bedtime/naga-page-05.mp3
Naga's Afternoon — 1

By afternoon, upstream rain had made the main river current stronger. Several of the magical festival flowers drifted away from their display. Mali looked upset. She wanted to hurry after them, but Naga reminded her that a powerful river deserved respect. Nobody should enter fast water to recover decorations.

The stronger current made the river look quite different from the gentle morning scene. Mali could see a cluster of magical flowers moving away, and for a moment the decorations seemed terribly important. Naga reminded her that the river was far more powerful than a floating blossom. The festival helpers remained on the safe path, looking for a place where the current would naturally carry the flowers into quieter water. Naga remembered the inlet and its spinning purple flower. There might be a way to recover the decorations without anybody going into the fast-moving river.

Naga's Afternoon — 2

Naga remembered the inlet from the morning and suggested that Mali and the grown-up festival helpers follow the familiar path along the bank. From a safe spot, they could see a cluster of colourful magical blossoms gathering among the reeds. The purple one was there too, dancing in the gentle water.

Afternoon · page 3
Target: audio/bedtime/naga-page-07.mp3
Naga's Afternoon — 3

The adult helpers recovered the decorations from the sheltered bank using their long festival baskets, while Mali stayed well away from the water's edge. Naga helped guide the enchanted flowers in our story world. Soon the baskets were full again, and Mali's favourite blossom rested safely on top like a little crown.

The adult helpers checked that the last enchanted flower had been collected. Mali clapped when the basket was brought back to the path. The decorations were a little damp, but that hardly mattered in a magical festival. They made sure none were left drifting away before returning to the village. Naga watched the reeds moving softly beside the inlet. The same river that had seemed so gentle in the morning had reminded everybody to pay attention, and they had found a way to celebrate while respecting it.

Naga's Evening — 1

As evening arrived, the villagers arranged the decorations on the festival platform. Colourful lanterns glowed beside the river, and their reflections trembled in the water. Mali thanked Naga for helping everyone celebrate without taking unnecessary risks. Naga admired the lights quietly from the sheltered inlet.

Evening · page 2
Target: audio/bedtime/naga-page-09.mp3
Naga's Evening — 2

The little sprite asked whether the river belonged to Naga. “No,” he answered in our tale. “We are all its neighbours.” They watched the water moving past the village. A river could be beautiful and powerful at the same time, and caring for it meant noticing what it needed as well as what people wanted.

Mali looked at the reflections of the lanterns and imagined the river flowing far beyond their little village, past other homes and forests. Naga listened to the water with her. They could enjoy its beauty without expecting it to do exactly what they wanted. The helpers would collect their festival decorations when the celebration ended, leaving the bank tidy for the next morning. Mali liked that idea. The beautiful little purple blossom could be a memory of the evening rather than something the river had to carry forever.

Naga's Evening — 3

The moon rose over the Mekong. Naga slipped beneath the peaceful waters of our imagination, leaving the festival lights shining above. Mali waved from the safe path, and the village grew quiet. The river's gentle song seemed to remind everyone that respect and kindness help us share the world. Goodnight, Naga.
